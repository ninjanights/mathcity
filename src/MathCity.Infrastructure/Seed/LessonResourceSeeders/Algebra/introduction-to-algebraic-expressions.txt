# Introduction to Algebraic Expressions

## What is an Algebraic Expression?

An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase made up of numbers, variables, and operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

Examples:
- x + 5
- 3a - 2
- 4m + 7n
- (x + y) / 2

Unlike equations, algebraic expressions do not contain an equals (=) sign.

## Components of an Expression

Variables:
Letters that represent unknown values.
Examples: x, y, a, b.

Constants:
Fixed numerical values.
Examples: 5, -3, 10.

Coefficients:
Numbers multiplied with variables.
Example:
In 7x, the coefficient is 7.

Terms:
Parts separated by addition or subtraction.

Example:
3x + 2y - 5

Terms:
- 3x
- 2y
- -5

## Types of Expressions

Monomial:
Contains one term.
Examples:
- 5x
- 8

Binomial:
Contains two terms.
Examples:
- x + 3
- 4a - 2

Trinomial:
Contains three terms.
Examples:
- x² + 3x + 2

Polynomial:
Contains multiple terms.

## Why Algebra Matters

Algebra allows us to represent unknown quantities, solve real-world problems, build mathematical models, and forms the foundation of higher mathematics, engineering, computer science, and physics.

## Summary

- Expressions have no equals sign.
- Variables represent unknown values.
- Constants never change.
- Coefficients multiply variables.
- Terms are separated by + or -.
